The reward pays the change, not the position

Each frame the game works out a “how good is this” number, and the frame’s reward is how much that number improved since the last frame, minus fuel. Sitting still earns nothing, however nice the spot. That detail is the whole ballgame.

score = −100 × distance − 100 × speed − 100 × |tilt| + 10 × legs down
reward = score − previous score − fuel (0.30 main, 0.03 side)
last frame = +100 at rest, or −100 for a crash

Why hovering pays

An episode is cut off at 1,000 frames. A bonus of +3 a frame is worth up to 3,000, while the main engine costs 0.30 a frame. Landing ends the episode and stops the payments, so the rocket learns to stop and hang there.

The fix

Pay the change in uprightness, γ·φ(s′) − φ(s), the way the built-in reward already pays the change in position — plus a small fee per frame. A closed loop nets to zero, so waiting earns nothing.

Reading the numbers

Positions are relative to the pad, tilt is in radians, and the last two are leg contact flags. The agent never sees the picture — only these eight numbers.
